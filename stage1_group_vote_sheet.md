# DATA1002 Stage 1 — Research Question Decision

**Decision (locked 2026-09-12): the group has selected rank 1, "How well does the
index represent the market?" (concentration, survivorship bias, constituent
turnover).**

The 15 questions below are retained as the record of options considered. They are
ranked from most to least promising in terms of how straightforward it is to
achieve high marks. The ranking considers:

- **Data availability** — free, citable, downloadable data (no paywalls)
- **Data-quality narrative** — whether the data has genuine issues to identify
  and justify cleaning decisions (a marking criterion worth 10% per member)
- **Slicing** — how easily the work splits into distinct pieces for 4–5 members
  (Sections B and C are individual)
- **Stakeholder clarity** — a clear "who cares and why" story (Section A)
- **Originality** — avoiding the most common student-project traps

---

## The ranked list

| Rank | Research question | Why this rank |
|:--:|:--|:--|
| 1 | **How well does the index represent the market?** — concentration, survivorship bias, constituent turnover | Highest ceiling: the survivorship-bias angle is original and feeds directly into the data-quality and analysis criteria. Requires a scope decision (current snapshot vs over time) |
| 2 | **Sector rotation across market conditions** — which sectors lead in different regimes | Safest strong pick: cleanest split across members (one sector each), a real classification-change story, easy data |
| 3 | **Should young Australians invest in the ASX?** — index vs individual stocks, dividends, volatility | Best stakeholder story for Section A; relatable to the cohort; solid data-quality narrative (adjusted vs unadjusted prices, survivorship) |
| 4 | **ASX vs US markets** — structure, sector mix, volatility, dividend culture | Easy data; honest medium cleaning story (currency, trading calendars, index construction); excellent slicing |
| 5 | **Who gets enough exercise?** — physical activity across the Australian population | Strongest non-finance cleaning story (a documented survey methodology change); excellent slicing by sport, state, age, sex |
| 6 | **The cost of the weekly shop** — groceries vs wages | Great data-literacy angle (index vs dollar prices); relatable; excellent slicing by food category and city |
| 7 | **How far have we come on pay equality?** — gender pay gap by industry and state | Strong stakeholder relevance; classic cleaning story (median vs mean, composition effects); great slicing |
| 8 | **Is the Australian dream of home ownership slipping away?** | Easy data and good slicing, but a common topic and the main datasets are comparatively clean (thinner quality narrative) |
| 9 | **How does Sydney move?** — commuting patterns by mode and region | Easy data with a genuine revision-caveat story; local relevance |
| 10 | **Has Australian tourism recovered?** — arrivals after COVID-19 | Easy data with a documented revision story; slightly less engaging topic |
| 11 | **Is Sydney's weather getting more extreme?** | Highest chart potential outside finance; deep history; but the source is professionally cleaned (subtler quality story) |
| 12 | **Is Sydney getting safer?** — recorded crime trends | Easy data and easy-to-find quality issues; but a grim topic and a common choice |
| 13 | **What drives student success at university?** | Easy data and medium cleaning story; but the stakeholder story is fuzzy and the topic is less engaging |
| 14 | **What makes a song popular?** | Fun with great slicing; but the data is pre-cleaned (thin quality narrative) and the stakeholder story is weak |
| 15 | **Stock-market seasonality — real or noise?** | Trivially easy data but the thinnest cleaning story and the weakest split across members |
